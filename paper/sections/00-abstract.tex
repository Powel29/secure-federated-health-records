\begin{abstract}
Accountable and transparent decryption gate key use on a log, and break-glass schemes for health records exist; but in the works we compare, the log-gated schemes have no patient revocation lifecycle, and the health-record schemes are not described as gating key release on a committed log entry.
% src: facts-F8#1, facts-F8#2, facts-F8#3, facts-F8#9, facts-F8#13, facts-F8#14, facts-F8#15, facts-F8#18, facts-F8#23
We implement a federated electronic health record prototype in which patient revocation, break-glass and expiry are signed, logged transitions of one key-lifecycle state machine over per-record keys, while consent is a patient-signed grant checked at read time when one is presented, outside the lifecycle; revocation and expiry re-key the record, and opening break-glass does not.
% src: facts-F1#1, facts-F1#11, facts-F1#35, facts-F1#46, facts-F1#47, facts-F1#77, facts-F1#78
Following transparent decryption, the audit service holds two of four shares of each record's emergency key and releases them only after validating a signed, one-time Emergency Assertion bound to the record and committing a hash-chained log entry.
% src: facts-F8#3, facts-F2#34, facts-F2#36, facts-F2#45, facts-F2#50, facts-F2#51
Emergency search, derived from that key, ends at the expiry rotation.
% src: facts-F3#51, facts-F3#53, facts-F3#54
On a simulated federation with synthetic data, all 35 system tests tagged to eleven security claims pass in a live run (test outcomes, not proofs), and packet captures show the audit commit before the shares.
% src: facts-F4#1, facts-F5#8, facts-live#12, facts-F6#43, facts-F6#49
Limitations: revocation is forward-only, expiry is server-enforced, not cryptographic, and the access structure is not full ciphertext-policy attribute-based encryption (not collusion-resistant).
% src: facts-F5#16, facts-F1#54, facts-F3#41
The audit service is trusted (its log has no proofs of presence or extension) and is a single point of availability, as the home authority's share replaces only a lost custodian share; ``no plaintext on the wire'' holds only under Transport Layer Security (TLS).

\end{abstract}
